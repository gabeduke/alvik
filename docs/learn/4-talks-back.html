📡 The robot talks back

So far the page talks and the robot listens. Now the robot starts talking back, and a 3D robot on the screen copies what the real one does. This is where it starts to feel like magic.

🧰 You need

  • The robot and remote from lesson 2, or the pretend robot.
🤖 Open the remote 🎮 Pretend robot

These links show only this lesson's part of the 3D robot: turning, wheels and lights.

▶️ Do it

  1. Put the robot on the floor pointing away from you, then connect. The 3D robot starts out pointing away from you too.
  2. Turn the robot on the spot with the joystick. The 3D robot turns the same way, and its wheels spin the way the real ones do.
  3. Press 💡 Lights a few times. The strips on the 3D robot take the colour of the real lights.
  4. Press 🪩 Disco and watch both of them dance.
  5. Walk round to the other side of the robot. Now left and right look swapped. Point the real robot away from you again and tap 🧭 to line the two up.

🔍 How it works

When the page connects it sends one more packet, a polite question: ! T 1, "please tell me what you are doing". From then on, about eight times a second, the robot sends back a ten-byte packet of news called telemetry. Here is one of the examples the code is tested with:

21start !54letter T7B 00heading17distance24lights41speed00turn03switches90checksum
BytesMeansIn this packet
7B 00Which way it points, in tenths of a degree. Turning left counts up.123, so 12.3° to the left
17What is in front, in cm (lesson 6). 255 means nothing.23 cm
24The lights: one bit each for red, green and blue, left then right.both blue
41Speed, in fifths of a cm/s.65, so 13 cm/s
00How fast it is turning, in steps of 3 degrees a second.not turning
03Switches: turbo, bumper, stopped by the bumper, dancing.turbo on, bumper on

Written in hexadecimal, the way programmers write bytes: 7B is 123 and 41 is 65.

Why squeeze it into ten bytes? A Bluetooth message of this kind holds about twenty, and the less the robot has to send, the more often it can send it. So every number is scaled to fit: the speed is counted in fifths so that one byte reaches 25 cm/s both ways.

The robot builds the packet in telemetry_packet() in remote_control.py, and the page takes it apart again in telemetry.js. Two programs in two languages have to agree on every byte. So there is one file of example packets, and both are tested against it.

How does it know which way it points?

The Alvik has no compass in this code. It counts how far each wheel has turned. If the left wheel has rolled further than the right one, the robot must have turned right. Working out where you are from your own wheels is called odometry. A robot vacuum cleaner does it, and so does a sailor counting knots.

It is not perfect, and that is worth seeing for yourself. Try the first two experiments below.

Why doesn't it lag?

News that arrives eight times a second would make the 3D robot turn in jerks. So between packets the page keeps turning it at the speed the robot last reported, and it draws the robot a tiny bit ahead of the last news, because by the time news arrives it is already old.

🧪 Try this

  • Fool it. Pick the real robot up and push the joystick left. The wheels spin in the air. What does the 3D robot do? (With the pretend robot, tap 🫳 to pick it up.)
  • Fool it the other way. Let go of the joystick, hold the robot in the air so its wheels don't turn, and turn round with it. Does the 3D robot notice? Why not?
  • Drive a square and come back to where you started. Is the 3D robot still lined up with the real one? Carpet and slippery floors make the wheels slip, and every slip is a small mistake that never gets fixed.
  • Read a packet. The lights byte above is 24, which is 36. In bits that is 100 100: read each group as blue, green, red. What would 09 be? (It is in the test examples: the robot stopped by the bumper.)

✏️ Change it

The page asks for news with telemetryRequest(true). An older remote, or the Bluefruit app, never asks, and the robot never sends: old and new keep working together. On the robot, TELEMETRY_MS = 100 sets how often it sends. Try 500 and watch the 3D robot fall behind, then catch up in steps.
