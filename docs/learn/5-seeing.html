👀 Seeing ahead

The distance sensor in the nose comes online as a cloud of dots in front of the 3D robot. It sees a lot, and it is often wrong.

🧰 You need

  • The robot and remote, or the pretend robot.
  • Your hand, a box, and some clear floor.
🤖 Open the remote 🎮 Pretend robot

▶️ Do it

  1. Connect, with the robot facing a wall a little way off. Each dot is one reading. (Pretend robot: drive toward the box.)
  2. Move your hand toward the nose. The dots follow.
  3. Move your hand left to right. The sensor sees five zones side by side.
  4. Face open floor. Do the dots go away?

No. With nothing ahead the dots sit about 17 cm away. The sensor tilts down and sees the floor.

🔍 How it works

It is a time-of-flight sensor: it flashes invisible light and times the bounce. Five zones across the front, plus one up and one down:

left, half_left, middle, half_right, right = alvik.get_distance()
top = alvik.get_distance_top()
bottom = alvik.get_distance_bottom()

The five go to the page in an S packet. Each dot fades after about a second, so a tight clump means steady readings and a smear means noise. Nothing past 40 cm gets a dot.

Two habits: it reads the floor at 16 to 18 cm when nothing is ahead, and a zone looking far away sometimes blips to the floor reading. Lesson 6 deals with both.

🧪 Try this

  • Thin things. How many zones see a pencil? A chair leg? A book?
  • Tricky things. A mirror, a black sock, a shiny pan. Which fool it?
  • Find the floor. Tip the nose up with a finger. Where do the dots go?

✏️ Peek with code

In Arduino Lab for MicroPython, connect, paste this, press Run, and wave your hand:

from arduino_alvik import ArduinoAlvik
from time import sleep_ms

alvik = ArduinoAlvik()
alvik.begin()

while True:
    print(alvik.get_distance())
    sleep_ms(200)

Press Stop, then restart the robot to get the menu back.
