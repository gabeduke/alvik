🕹️ Drive it

Connect a phone or laptop to the robot over Bluetooth and drive it around. Then look at what the page really sends: five bytes at a time.

🧰 You need

  • The robot from lesson 1, on the floor with some room.
  • A phone, tablet or laptop with Chrome or Edge. On an iPad or iPhone, the free Bluefy browser. (Safari and Firefox can't do Bluetooth from a web page.)
🤖 Open the remote 🎮 No robot: use the pretend one

These links open the remote with the sensors switched off, so there is less to look at. You will see a 3D robot copying yours: how it knows what to do is lesson 4.

▶️ Do it

  1. On the robot, tap ⬆ until the lights are purple, then tap ✓. The lights go yellow: it is waiting for you.
  2. On the remote, press Connect and pick Alvik. The robot's lights go white.
  3. Drag the joystick. Push a little to go slowly and a lot to go fast.
  4. Try the four buttons: 💡 Lights changes the colour, 🚀 Turbo is faster, 🪩 Disco dances, and 🛡️ Bumper is lesson 6.
  5. On a laptop you can use the arrow keys or W A S D, and 1 to 4 for the buttons.
  6. To stop, press ✗ on the robot. It goes back to the menu.

Only one remote at a time. While someone is connected the robot stops calling out, so nobody else can find it. Yellow lights mean it is calling out again.

🔍 How it works

The robot and the page talk over Bluetooth Low Energy. The robot calls out "I'm Alvik", the page answers, and from then on the page can write short messages to it. Nothing travels over the internet: you can switch the wifi off and it still works.

Every message is a packet of five bytes. A byte is a number from 0 to 255.

!startBbutton3which one11 down, 0 up8checksum
!startJjoystickx-100 to 100y-100 to 100?checksum

Shown as letters here. Each letter is really a number: ! is 33, B is 66, 1 is 49.

A button packet says which button, and whether it went down or came back up. A joystick packet says where the stick is: x is left and right, y is back and forward.

The last byte is a checksum. The page adds up the first four bytes, flips every bit, and sends what is left. The robot does the same sum on what arrived. If its answer is different, the radio garbled something, and it throws the packet away instead of driving off on a wrong number.

Make a packet

On the robot, remote_control.py picks packets out of whatever arrives. This is the check:

pkt = buf[i:i + 5]
if (~sum(pkt[:4])) & 0xFF != pkt[4]:
    buf = buf[i + 1:]                  # damaged: look for the next '!'
    continue

We did not invent the button packets. They are what the free Bluefruit Connect app from Adafruit sends from its Control Pad, so the robot can be driven from that app as well. The joystick packet is our own.

🧪 Try this

  • Do a checksum by hand. Lights down is ! B 1 1. As numbers that is 33, 66, 49 and 49. Add them up. Take the answer away from 255. Is it what the packet maker shows?
  • Find the joystick's zero. In the packet maker, slide the stick to -1. What number does the byte show? Bytes can't be negative, so -1 is sent as 255, -2 as 254, and so on.
  • Use two hands. Tap 🙌 Two hands at the top of the remote: left thumb for speed, right thumb for steering. Both layouts send the very same packet.
  • Walk away. Carry the phone out of the room while the robot is driving. What does the robot do when Bluetooth can't reach any more? (Lesson 3 explains.)
  • Try the app. Install Bluefruit Connect, connect to Alvik, then Controller, then Control Pad. Same robot code, different remote.
