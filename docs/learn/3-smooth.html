🚀 Smooth moves

A robot that jumps to full speed tips, skids and is hard to steer. This one ramps: it speeds up gently and slows down a bit quicker. It also stops by itself if the remote goes quiet.

🧰 You need

  • The robot and remote from lesson 2, or the pretend robot.
🤖 Open the remote 🎮 Pretend robot

▶️ Do it

  1. Connect, then push the joystick all the way forward in one go. The robot takes about a quarter of a second to reach its speed of 8 cm/s (the 💨 number under the 3D robot). That is short, but it is not a jump: watch how it doesn't jerk.
  2. Let go. It stops faster than it started.
  3. Push forward, then pull straight back. It slows to zero first and only then goes backwards.
  4. Press 🚀 Turbo and do it again. The top speed is now 13 cm/s, about as fast as an Alvik goes.
  5. Now push the stick just a little. Nothing happens in the very middle: that is the dead zone, so a resting thumb doesn't creep the robot along.

🔍 How it works

Roughly thirty times a second the robot goes round a loop: read what was asked for, work out a speed, tell the wheels. The joystick says what speed you want. The robot keeps its own number for the speed it is doing now, and each time round the loop it moves that number a small step towards what you want.

ACCEL = 30           # cm/s per second: how gently it speeds up
DECEL = 60           # cm/s per second: how quickly it slows down

linear = ramp(linear, want_linear, ACCEL * dt, DECEL * dt)

dt is how long the last trip round the loop took, in seconds. So one step is "30 cm/s per second, times a thirtieth of a second": about 1 cm/s. About eight steps to reach full speed.

If the remote goes quiet

What if the phone's battery dies while the robot is driving at the stairs? While you hold the joystick, the page sends its packet again about five times a second, even if you haven't moved. The robot expects that. If it hears nothing for 600 milliseconds, it stops:

if stick != (0, 0) and ticks_diff(now, stick_at) > STICK_TIMEOUT_MS:
    stick = (0, 0)                 # the page stopped talking: don't keep driving

Engineers call this a dead man's switch. Trains have one too. The page helps as well: if you switch to another app or the screen locks, it lets go of the joystick for you.

🧪 Try this

  • Time it. From the numbers above: how long does it take to get from 0 to 8 cm/s? And from 8 back to 0? Check with the graph.
  • Make a truck. In the graph, slide "speeding up" down to 5. How would that robot feel to drive? What about "slowing down" at 5 near the edge of a table?
  • Test the switch. Drive the real robot forward and walk away with the phone until it stops. Two things can stop it: Bluetooth noticing that the phone has gone, or 600 milliseconds going by with no packet. Why is it good to have both?
  • Find the dead zone. In two-hands mode, push the speed slider up very slowly. How far does it go before the robot moves?

✏️ Change it

The numbers that set how the robot drives are at the top of remote_control.py: SPEED, TURBO_SPEED, TURN, ACCEL, DECEL. To change one on your robot:

  1. Plug the robot in and open Arduino Lab for MicroPython. Connect, and open the robot's files.
  2. Open remote_control.py from the robot, change ACCEL = 30 to ACCEL = 8, and save it back to the robot.
  3. Switch the robot off and on, go to purple, and drive.

The update page from lesson 1 puts the original numbers back. To keep your changes for good, make your own copy of the project: lesson 9.
