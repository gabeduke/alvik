🛡️ The bumper

The robot should stop before it drives into the wall. That sounds like one line of code. It took us several tries. This lesson is about turning noisy readings into a decision.

🧰 You need

  • The robot and remote, or the pretend robot.
  • A wall or a big box, a chair, and a clear bit of floor.
🤖 Open the remote 🎮 Pretend robot

▶️ Do it

  1. Check that 🛡️ Bumper says "on". Drive straight at a wall from a metre away and keep the joystick pushed.
  2. About 30 cm from the wall the robot starts to slow down, and its lights blink red slowly. On the screen, a sheet of heat appears in front of the 3D robot: yellow, then orange.
  3. At 12 cm it stops. The lights flash red fast and the heat turns red. The 🧱 number is what the robot thinks is in front of it.
  4. Pull the joystick back. Reversing always works: the bumper only cares about what is ahead.
  5. Now drive at a chair leg. It slows down less than it did for the wall, and gets much closer. The real robot bumps into it. (The pretend one stops about 4 cm away.) Hmm.
  6. Turn the bumper off and drive at the wall again (gently). Nothing saves you now.

🔍 How it works

The dots from lesson 5 are what the sensor reads. The heat is what the robot has decided. Getting from one to the other is the hard part.

First idea: stop for the closest reading

if min(alvik.get_distance()) < 12:
    stop()

You know from lesson 5 why this fails. The floor reads 17 cm, so a robot that slows down for anything under 30 cm crawls across an empty room, blinking red at nothing. And every blip is a jolt.

Second idea: ignore the floor's distance

Throw away anything near 17 cm? Then a real wall goes invisible for a moment as the robot drives up to it, right when it matters.

What it does now

The robot looks at each of the middle three zones on its own and asks three questions.

  1. Is it a blip? A thing has to show up four readings in a row before it counts.
  2. Is it the floor? A reading near where the floor shows only counts if the robot has watched it come closer while driving at it. A wall comes closer. The floor doesn't: it stays 17 cm ahead however far you drive.
  3. Has it stopped coming closer? If the robot drives 4 cm at a "thing" and it comes no closer, it was floor after all (a ramp, or a thick carpet), and the robot remembers that this is where the floor shows now.

Then it takes the average of the three zones, not the closest, counting a clear zone as 30 cm. And it smooths that: each time round the loop it moves only part of the way to the new answer.

total = 0
for zone, d in zip(self.zones, distances[1:4]):
    d = zone.see(d, moved, turned)
    total += SLOW_CM if d is None or d > SLOW_CM else d
self.ahead += (total / 3 - self.ahead) * BUMPER_SMOOTH

Last, that one distance sets a speed limit: full speed beyond 30 cm, slower and slower down to 12 cm, and zero inside 12 cm.

Closest, or average?

Set all three to 20: a wall. Both rules agree. Now set just the middle one to 6 and leave the others at 60: a chair leg. The closest rule stops. The average rule only slows down, and the robot carries on towards the leg.

So why use the average? Because the closest rule also stops for every blip and every wobble, and a robot that stops for nothing is no fun to drive. We chose smooth driving and a gentle bump now and then. That is a trade-off, and engineers make them all the time. You might choose differently.

🧪 Try this

  • Measure it. Drive at a wall and let the bumper stop the robot. Measure from its nose to the wall with a ruler. Is it 12 cm? Do it five times. Is it the same each time?
  • Come in at an angle. Drive at the wall slanting, not straight on. Does it still stop in time? Which zones see the wall first?
  • Dots against heat. On open floor, look at the dots (17 cm, the floor) and at the heat (nothing). That gap is this whole lesson.
  • Invent a rule. What about "the closest of the three, but only if it stays there for four readings"? Use the sliders to think it through. What would it do at the chair leg? On a ramp?

✏️ Change it

In remote_control.py, SLOW_CM = 30 and BUMPER_CM = 12 set where it slows and where it stops. For a faster robot you might want to start slowing down sooner. The whole of the bumper is two small classes there, Zone and Bumper, with a comment on every rule. Lesson 3 says how to change a file on the robot.
