🌈 Underneath

The last sensors come online: the ones that look down, and the one that feels the robot being tipped. The 3D robot now shows the colour of the floor, black lines, and whether someone has picked the robot up.

🧰 You need

  • The robot and remote, or the pretend robot.
  • Sheets of coloured paper (red, green and blue are best), white paper, and black tape or a thick black pen.
🤖 Open the remote 🎮 Pretend robot

Everything is switched on from here.

▶️ Do it

  1. Connect. Under the 3D robot there is now a round patch of floor. It is the colour the robot's colour sensor sees underneath it.
  2. Drive onto a sheet of red paper. The patch turns red. Try the other colours.
  3. Look at the 3D robot's nose: three small rings. They are the line sensors. Over a pale floor they are bright.
  4. Stick a strip of black tape on white paper and drive across it slowly. The rings go dark one after the other, or all at once if you cross it straight on.
  5. Pick the robot up. The 3D robot rises and leaves its shadow on the floor.
  6. Tip it forwards, backwards and to each side. The 3D robot leans the same way.
  7. Find the 🔋 number: the robot's battery.

With the pretend robot: drive over the tape and onto the three mats, and tap 🫳 to pick it up.

🔍 How it works

A third packet follows the other two, with the letter U for under:

!startUunder▮▮▮three line sensors▮▮▮floor: red, green, blue▮low distance▮high distance▮checksum

Line sensors

Under the nose are three small sensors in a row. Each one shines a light down and measures how much comes back. A pale floor bounces a lot back, and the number is low: about 50 on our desk. Black tape soaks the light up, and the number is high. So does no floor at all.

The blue program in the robot's menu, the line follower, uses just these three numbers. If the left sensor sees more black than the right one, the line is off to the left, so it steers left.

Colour sensor

Also underneath is a sensor that measures red, green and blue light separately. The Alvik library gives each as a number from 0 to 1:

red, green, blue = alvik.get_color()

The page paints the patch with exactly those three numbers. A screen makes its colours out of red, green and blue as well, so nothing needs converting.

Picked up

There is no "I am being carried" sensor. But remember the distance sensor's low zone from lesson 5: standing on the floor it sees the floor just ahead, about 7 cm away. If that number suddenly grows, the floor has gone further away, and the likely reason is that somebody lifted the robot. The higher the number, the higher the 3D robot floats.

That is a sensor being used for something it was not built for. Engineers do this a lot.

Tipped

Inside the Alvik is a chip that feels which way is down and how fast the robot is turning, like the one that tells a phone it has been turned sideways. From it come two angles: roll (leaning to a side) and pitch (nose up or down). They ride in the S packet from lesson 5. The page ignores a lean of less than 4 degrees, or the 3D robot would wobble on every bump.

A letter for each kind of news

The robot now sends three packets, T, S and U, eight times a second. Each was added later than the one before, and each time there were robots and pages out there with the older code. The rule that kept them all working: never change a packet, add a new letter. An old page skips letters it doesn't know. A new page copes with a robot that never sends them.

🧪 Try this

  • Colour hunt. Drive around the house. What colour does the robot think the carpet is? The kitchen floor? A wooden table? Is it what you would call it?
  • Hard colours. Try yellow, orange, pink and brown paper. Which come out wrong? What do white and black look like?
  • How thin? Draw lines of different widths. What is the thinnest line that turns a ring dark?
  • The edge of the table. Hold the robot (don't drive it!) with its nose poking over a table edge. What do the three rings show, and why? Could a robot use that to keep itself from falling off?
  • Lift or cliff? The low distance grows when the robot is lifted. When else would it grow? How could the robot tell the difference?

✏️ Peek with code

Read the downward sensors yourself in Arduino Lab for MicroPython, as in lesson 5. Slide coloured paper under the robot and lift it up while this runs:

from arduino_alvik import ArduinoAlvik
from time import sleep_ms

alvik = ArduinoAlvik()
alvik.begin()

while True:
    print("lines", alvik.get_line_sensors(),
          "colour", alvik.get_color(),
          "floor", alvik.get_distance_bottom())
    sleep_ms(300)

How dark a line has to be before the rings go dark, and how far a lift raises the 3D robot, are numbers in sensing.js. Some of them are still our best guess: use what your own robot prints to set them better.
